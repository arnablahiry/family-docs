Privacy policy

Lararium (Android app) · Last updated 29 October 2026

In short

Lararium keeps your family's documents on your phone, encrypted. Nothing is sent to the developer. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

What the app stores on your phone

  • The documents, photos, details and logins you add, the people and groups you create, and your settings.
  • All of it is stored in an encrypted database and encrypted files, protected by your passcode / fingerprint and the phone's secure key storage.

Google sign-in and Google Drive

If you choose "Continue with Google", the app asks Google for:

  • Your name, email address and profile photo, to show on your account and to know which Drive holds your backup.
  • Access to files the app creates in your Google Drive (drive.file) and to the app's hidden app folder (drive.appdata). The app cannot see any other files in your Drive.

Backups are written to a "Lararium" folder in your own Google Drive. Documents and logins are encrypted before they leave the phone; the backup key is kept in the app's hidden folder in your Drive so you can restore on a new phone. Logins are always encrypted. You can delete the backup from the app at any time (Settings), or revoke the app's access in your Google Account.

Lararium's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to provide the backup, restore and group-sharing features you ask for, is not transferred to anyone else, and is never used for advertising.

Sharing a group with other people

If you invite someone to a group, your phone puts an encrypted copy of your own and the group's shared documents in your Drive, shared by link. It can only be read with the group's code, which travels inside the invite you send. You decide who gets an invite.

Join requests

To let someone ask to join a group with just its code, the app uses a small Google Firebase database as a mailbox, with an anonymous sign-in (no name or email). What it stores there is encrypted: the group is found by a hash of its code, names are sealed with the code, and the reply to a request is sealed to the requester's phone. Requests can be deleted by the person who sent them.

What the developer receives

Nothing. The developer has no server that receives your documents, details, logins or usage.

Updates

The app checks GitHub (github.com) for a newer release and, if you agree, downloads it from there.

Deleting your data

Settings › Delete account removes everything from the phone and, if you choose, the backup from your Drive. You can also revoke Lararium's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Contact
